## BLUF

Russia is conducting material military preparation around Ukraine, with reported movements towards forward staging strengthening concern that it is maintaining an option for overt action. The evidence warrants senior attention but does not establish that Moscow has decided to attack. [A13, A33, A39]

## Key judgements

**Reported movement towards Ukraine is more discriminating than the existence of large concentrations.** Geolocated video reporting on 11 February described elements previously around Yelnya moving south towards Klintsy and equipment moving by road beyond rail-delivered concentration areas. The analyst assesses this as consistent with forward staging; intended employment remains unresolved. [A23, A33]

**The reported changes extend across several approaches.** The analyst's review of commercially released Maxar imagery from 9-10 February describes more than 550 troop tents and hundreds of vehicles at Oktyabrskoye in Crimea, troops and helicopters at Zyabrovka in Belarus, and continued arrivals at Kursk. These findings strengthen the assessment of material preparation beyond routine activity, without establishing an attack decision. [A13, A24, A32]

**Government precautions reinforce concern.** On 11 February the UK advised nationals to leave while commercial transport remained available. By 12 February the US had ordered most remaining embassy personnel out of Kyiv and suspended normal consular operations. The analyst regards these costly actions as more significant than rhetoric, while noting that public US statements did not establish a final Russian decision. [A25, A27, A34]

## Why it matters

The original anomaly now sits alongside reported changes in military posture. Preparation for overt action is sufficiently plausible and consequential to warrant senior awareness and priority collection. This strengthens concern about the physical environment; it does not establish that military preparation caused the financial or digital anomalies. [A36, A39]

## Alternatives and uncertainty

Reversible preparation remains a good fit: forces can support military action while Moscow retains the option not to use them. Announced Russian-Belarusian exercises and coercive signalling remain plausible components, although the analyst judges reported forward movements difficult to explain wholly as exercises. Moscow's denial of an intention to invade is relevant counterevidence but does not resolve the conflict between declared intent and observed posture. [A13, A16, A18, A35]

## Outlook and indicators to watch

The key question is whether forces move from concentration into a posture permitting operations with little additional preparation. Dispersal from camps and railheads, expanded field medical, fuel and ammunition support, or simultaneous forward movement across several approaches would raise concern. Verified withdrawals, dismantled accommodation and reversed forward movements would reduce it. Continue priority collection; the assessment establishes neither an attack decision nor its timing. [A19, A40, A42, A43, A45]

## Analytical confidence

Moderate confidence in material military preparation beyond routine peacetime activity; intent to employ the forces remains unresolved. [A13]

## Source assessment

This synthesis relies on the analyst's descriptions of commercial imagery, geolocated public reporting and government measures. It does not independently inspect imagery or establish source independence. Sentinel catalogue entries are not observed deployments, and unavailable SAR/VIIRS coverage cannot support a negative finding about preparation. [A22-A30, A46-A47]
